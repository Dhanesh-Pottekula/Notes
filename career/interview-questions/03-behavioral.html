Interview questions · 03

Behavioral, leadership and HR rounds

219 questions · L1 69 · L2 110 · L3 40 · 13 sub-topics

Startups and product companies give these rounds real weight: they check ownership, judgement and whether people want to work with you. Prepare 8–10 STAR stories (2 minutes each, a number in the result) and map every question below to one of them; the hints name the story from your own work that fits. Story bank: ../interview-topics/12-behavioral.md.

3.1 Tell me about yourself — your career story

Learn: ../application-answers.md "Tell us about yourself (60 seconds)" · ../interview-topics/12-behavioral.md "12.2 Common questions" · techinterviewhandbook.org/self-introduction/

  • L1 Tell me about yourself.
    now → before → why this role; 60–90 seconds, one number
  • L1 Walk me through your resume, job by job.
  • L1 You studied civil engineering. How did you end up in software?
    self-taught from 2023, projects, StreetDine in final year
  • L1 What does your current role involve, day to day?
  • L1 Describe yourself in three words, with an example for each.
  • L2 Why did you join StreetDine while still in college?
    founding team, real users, learn by shipping
  • L2 Why did you move from StreetDine to Jigsaw?
  • L2 Why did you move from Jigsaw to AiRA?
    founding role, AI-first product, more ownership
  • L2 What did each job teach you that the previous one didn't?
  • L2 How did you learn backend engineering without a CS degree? Which gaps did you have to close?
    name each gap and how you closed it
  • L2 Does your civil engineering background help you as an engineer? How?
  • L2 Which one project best shows how you work?
    Lumen or the AiRA voice rewrite
  • L2 Tell me your story as if I were a non-technical founder.
  • L3 Your degree isn't in CS. Why should we trust your fundamentals?
    shipped systems, open source, concepts you can be drilled on
  • L3 Three companies in under three years. Why should we believe you'll stay?
    a clear reason per move; what you want long term
  • L3 What would your Jigsaw manager say about you? What would your CEO at AiRA say?

3.6 Production incidents, deadlines, prioritisation and ambiguity

Learn: sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/ · staffeng.com/guides/work-on-what-matters · AIE ch1 "Milestone Planning" (pdf p57)

  • L1 Tell me about a production incident you handled.
  • L1 How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?
  • L2 Walk me through your first 15 minutes when production breaks.
    impact, tell people, mitigate or roll back, then root cause
  • L2 How do you write a postmortem? What makes it blameless?
  • L2 Tell me about a time a fix for one bug caused another.
  • L2 Tell me about a time you were paged late at night. What did you change afterwards?
  • L2 How do you choose between a quick patch and a proper fix?
  • L2 Two deadlines clash. How do you choose?
  • L2 A deadline is impossible. What do you say, and to whom?
    early, with options: cut scope, move date, accept risk
  • L2 Tell me about a time the requirements changed halfway through.
  • L2 You get a vague request. How do you turn it into a plan?
    user, success metric, smallest version, a checkpoint
  • L2 How do you decide what not to build?
  • L2 How do you start work nobody on the team has done before?
    Lumen or realtime voice: read, spike, prototype, measure
  • L3 Something breaks in the middle of an investor demo. Walk me through it.
  • L3 How do you get tech-debt work onto the roadmap?
  • L3 How do you estimate work that is mostly unknown?
    spike first, ranges not points, re-estimate at checkpoints

3.7 Team lead and founding engineer

  • L1 How big is your team, and what does each person work on?

  • L2 How do you give hard feedback to someone on your team? Give a real example.

  • L2 How do you say no to a request without damaging the relationship?
  • L2 How do you onboard a new engineer into a fast-moving codebase?
  • L2 Have you interviewed or hired anyone? How do you run an interview?
    a task close to the real job; rubric; look at reasoning
  • L2 How do you split work across a 4-person frontend and backend team?
  • L2 How do you keep code quality up when everyone is moving fast?
    review rules, CI, tests on risky paths, clear owners
  • L3 Two engineers want to build the same thing in two different ways. How do you decide?

3.8 Working with AI tools

Learn: anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-best-practices · docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/sub-agents · simonwillison.net/tags/ai-assisted-programming/

  • L1 Which AI coding tools do you use, and for what?
  • L1 What do you never let an AI tool do without checking?
  • L2 Walk me through building a feature with Claude Code.
    spec → plan → task-sized commits → review each diff → tests
  • L2 How do you review AI-written code? What do you look for first?
    wrong assumptions, deleted tests, stale docs, security, edge cases
  • L2 Tell me about a time an AI tool produced something wrong that looked right.
    docs describing a deleted Redux layer; tests removed in a commit
  • L2 Some of your side projects were mostly agent-built. How do you show you own that code?
    you wrote the specs, reviewed diffs, can defend each design
  • L2 How do you keep secrets and customer data out of AI tools?
  • L2 How do you use subagents or several agents at once?
    learning-os: one agent per job, a checker agent verifies
  • L2 Has AI made you faster? How do you know?
    honest: faster to draft, slower to review
  • L2 How do you set rules for AI tools across a team?
    AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md conventions, review standards, no blind merges
  • L2 How do you stop juniors from shipping AI code they don't understand?
  • L3 An agent writes 17,000 lines in one commit. How do you review it?
  • L3 Would you let an AI agent merge to main? Under what conditions?
  • L3 How do you write a spec so an agent builds the right thing?
    goal, constraints, examples, acceptance tests, small tasks
  • L3 Where will AI coding tools be in two years, and how does that change what you learn now?

3.12 Salary, notice period and HR trick questions

Learn: ../application-brief.md "Numbers to enter" · techinterviewhandbook.org/negotiation/ · techinterviewhandbook.org/understanding-compensation/

  • L1 What is your current CTC?
    state it plainly; split fixed and variable
  • L1 What is your expected CTC?
    give a range; ask their band first
  • L1 What is your notice period? Can you join earlier?
    state it; mention early release if possible
  • L1 Are you open to relocating? Which cities?
  • L1 Are you open to working from the office five days a week?
  • L2 Why is your expectation so much higher than your current salary?
    market rate for the role, scope, other offers; stay calm
  • L2 Our budget is below your range. What now?
    ask total comp: ESOPs, bonus, review date; know your floor
  • L2 How do you compare a startup offer with ESOPs against a higher fixed salary?
    vesting, cliff, strike price, liquidity, dilution
  • L2 Do you have other offers?
    honest; share timelines, not necessarily numbers
  • L2 If your company makes a counter-offer, will you take it?
    you decided on reasons, not money; say so
  • L2 Your GitHub shows StreetDine commits during your Jigsaw dates. Explain.
    part-time on StreetDine after Aug 2024; one honest line
  • L2 You joined StreetDine before finishing your degree. How did you manage both?
  • L2 Your resume shows nothing between April and August 2024. What were you doing?
    graduation, job search, side projects from that window
  • L2 Why three jobs in under three years?
    one line per move; moving towards something, not away
  • L2 Tell me something about you that isn't on your resume.
  • L2 Can you share payslips and relieving letters for background verification?
    keep offer letters, relieving letters, payslips, degree ready
  • L2 Why should we not hire you?
  • L2 You lead a team now. Will you be fine in an individual-contributor role?
    yes, with reasons; leading without the title
  • L3 You've accepted another offer and we come back with a better one. What do you do?
  • L3 Would you sign a service bond or accept a long notice period?
  • L3 Your manager asks you to do something you think is unethical. What do you do?
  • L3 Rate yourself out of 10 in Python and in system design.
    honest, with evidence; never a 10
