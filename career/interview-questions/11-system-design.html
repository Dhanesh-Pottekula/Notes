System design — concepts

535 questions · L1 134 · L2 271 · L3 130 · 30 sub-topics

Every product company and most startups run a 45–60 minute design round; at 3 years you must drive it and defend each building block (load balancer, cache, replicas, shards, queues, consensus) with trade-offs and numbers. This file is the concept bank; full problems are in 29-system-design-problems.md. Syllabus: 2C System design (../../syllabus.html).

11.2 Back-of-envelope estimation and numbers to know

Learn: System Design Interview (Xu) vol1 ch2 · gist.github.com/jboner/2841832 (latency numbers) · github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer#powers-of-two-table · DDIA ch1 "Describing load" (pdf p31)

  • L1 How many seconds in a day, roughly? Why do people round it to 10⁵?
  • L1 1 million requests per day is about how many QPS?
    ~12 QPS
  • L1 What is the usual ratio of peak to average traffic?
    2–3×, more for spiky events
  • L1 Powers of two: what are 2¹⁰, 2²⁰, 2³⁰, 2⁴⁰ in KB/MB/GB/TB terms?
  • L1 Latency numbers: L1 cache, RAM, SSD read, same-datacenter round trip, disk seek, cross-continent round trip.
    0.5 ns, 100 ns, ~100 µs, 0.5 ms, 10 ms, 150 ms
  • L1 How much downtime per year does 99.9% allow? 99.99%? 99.999%?
    ~8.8 h, ~53 min, ~5 min
  • L1 Roughly how many simple QPS can one Postgres node serve? One Redis node? One app server?
    ~5–10k, ~100k ops/s, ~1–5k RPS
  • L2 100M DAU, each user does 10 reads and 1 write a day. What are read QPS, write QPS and peak QPS?
  • L2 10M new posts a day at 1 KB each plus 10% with a 500 KB image. What is storage per day and per 5 years?
  • L2 How do you estimate bandwidth (ingress and egress) from QPS and payload size?
  • L2 How do you estimate how many servers you need? What do you assume per server?
  • L2 How do you estimate cache memory for "the hottest 20% of a day's reads"?
  • L2 How do you compute availability of two services in series? In parallel?
    series multiply; parallel 1 − product of failure rates
  • L2 How many WebSocket connections can one server hold, and what limits it?
    memory per connection, file descriptors, CPU for heartbeats
  • L2 What does an 80/20 rule mean in cache sizing, and when is it wrong?
  • L2 Why does reading 1 MB sequentially from memory vs SSD vs network vs disk matter for design?
  • L3 Your estimate says 2 servers are enough. Why might you still run 6?
  • L3 How do you estimate LLM cost per month for 1M users sending 20 messages a day?
  • L3 Estimate the number of GPUs needed to serve 1,000 concurrent streaming LLM sessions. What numbers do you need?
  • L3 The interviewer says your numbers are off by 10×. Which parts of your design change and which don't?

11.6 Caching

    browser, CDN, gateway, app in-memory, distributed (Redis), DB buffer pool
  • L1 What is cache hit ratio? What is a good one?
  • L1 What is TTL?
  • L1 Name the eviction policies.
    LRU, LFU, FIFO, random, TTL-based; Redis allkeys-lru etc.
  • L2 Cache-aside vs read-through vs write-through vs write-behind vs write-around: explain each.
  • L2 Which caching pattern would you use for a user profile, a product catalogue, a leaderboard, a session?
  • L2 What do you cache and what do you never cache?

  • L2 What is cache penetration (requests for keys that don't exist)? Fix?

  • L2 What is cache avalanche (many keys expire at once)? Fix?

  • L3 How do you keep a cache consistent with the database without dual writes?
    CDC-driven invalidation (Debezium → invalidate)
  • L3 Can a cache make your system less available? How?
    cold cache after restart overloads DB
  • L3 How do you warm a cache after a deploy or failover?
  • L3 What is semantic caching for LLM responses? When is it dangerous?

  • L3 What did you cache in Redis at AiRA, what TTLs did you choose, and what went wrong?
