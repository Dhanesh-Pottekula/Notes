System design — high level (P0)

At 3 years you are expected to drive a 45-minute design: requirements → estimates → API → data model → high-level diagram → deep dive on 2 parts → bottlenecks and trade-offs. Practise out loud with a timer, drawing on excalidraw.com.

6.2 Numbers to know

  • Latency: L1 0.5 ns, RAM 100 ns, SSD read ~100 µs, same-DC round trip 0.5 ms, disk seek 10 ms, cross-continent 150 ms
    gist.github.com/jboner/2841832 · colin-scott.github.io/personal_website/research/interactive_latency.html · DDIA ch8 "Timeouts and unbounded delays" (pdf p295)
  • Powers of two: 2¹⁰ ≈ 1 thousand (KB), 2²⁰ ≈ 1 million (MB), 2³⁰ ≈ 1 billion (GB), 2⁴⁰ ≈ 1 trillion (TB)
    github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer#powers-of-two-table · Xu vol1 ch2 "Back-of-the-envelope Estimation" (book, not in library)
  • One Postgres node: ~5–10k simple QPS; Redis ~100k ops/s; one app server ~1–5k RPS
    hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/core-concepts/numbers-to-know (members-only) · DDIA ch1 "Approaches for coping with load" (pdf p37) · Xu vol1 ch2 (book, not in library)
  • 1 day ≈ 86,400 s ≈ 10⁵ s; 1M requests/day ≈ 12 QPS
    Xu vol1 ch2 "Back-of-the-envelope Estimation" (book, not in library) · DDIA ch1 "Describing load" (pdf p31) · Do: estimate QPS + storage for 3 problems in 6.9
  • Availability: 99.9% ≈ 8.8 h/year down, 99.99% ≈ 53 min/year
    sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/ · github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer#availability-patterns ("Availability in numbers") · DDIA ch1 "Reliability" (pdf p26)
