How to Win Friends and Influence People

One study page per chapter, in the book's order, to read instead of the book. Each page states the chapter's principle, maps how its argument fits together, retells every story in plain words, and adds a "Picture it" analogy, what's happening behind it, how it looks at work, a before/after rewrite, small exercises to try, where it can go wrong, and a "check yourself" quiz.

How the whole book fits together

Part One: stop hurting, start valuing→Part Two: be liked→Part Three: be agreed with→Part Four: change people as a leader

Part One lays the base: people defend themselves when criticized, crave feeling important, and act only on what they want. Part Two turns that into six habits that make people like you: interest, a smile, their name, listening, their interests, making them feel important. Part Three is about persuasion without fights: avoid arguments, respect their view, admit your mistakes, begin friendly, get early yeses, let them talk and own the idea, see their side, appeal to nobler motives, dramatize, challenge. Part Four applies it all to correcting people without resentment.

The thread through every chapter: the other person's feelings and wants decide what they do, so start from them, and be sincere — the book warns again and again that technique without sincerity is flattery and fails.
