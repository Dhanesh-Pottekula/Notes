Your Personal Roadmap

From Zero to Beautiful Digital Paintings

A phased plan covering what to learn, what to buy, and when — built around your Android phone and Mac M4 laptop.

Your Starting Point

Android phone, Mac M4 laptop, mouse. No drawing tablet, no stylus, no art experience needed yet. This roadmap starts exactly where you are.

Phase 1

Learn to See Like an Artist

Weeks 1–4 · Cost: ₹0 · Use what you already have

Before you buy anything, you need to train the most important tool you already own: your eyes. Great painting starts with learning to see — how light falls on objects, how shapes fit together, how shadows create depth. This costs nothing and makes everything you do later dramatically better.

Start with pencil and paper. Any notebook, any pencil. Draw for 30 minutes every day. It doesn't matter if it looks terrible — every artist's first hundred drawings look terrible, and that's normal. The goal is to train your hand to follow your eye.

What to Study (Free Resources)

Drawabox.com — Start here. This free structured course teaches you the absolute fundamentals: drawing lines, boxes, ellipses, and building 3D forms. It's boring at first, but it builds the foundation everything else sits on. Do Lesson 1 and the 250 Box Challenge.

Ctrl+Paint (ctrlpaint.com) — 80+ free video lessons by concept artist Matt Kohr. Start with the "Traditional Drawing" and "Digital Painting 101" tracks. These teach value, form, and light in a way that applies to any software.

Proko (YouTube) — Excellent free figure drawing and anatomy lessons. Start with the "Basics" playlist. His teaching style is fun and clear.

📖 Book to Read Now

"Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain"
by Betty Edwards
The single best starting book for someone who has never drawn before. It doesn't teach you "how to draw a cat" step by step — instead, it rewires how your brain sees things. It teaches you to stop drawing what you think something looks like and start drawing what you actually see. Many people who believed they "couldn't draw" produced shockingly good work after just working through this book. Read it alongside your daily pencil practice.
~₹500–800

Daily Practice — 30 Minutes

10 min: Warm up with Drawabox exercises — draw lines, curves, and boxes to train your hand-eye coordination and line confidence.

20 min: Draw something from real life. Your coffee mug, your shoe, your hand, a fruit on the table. Look at it more than you look at the paper. Focus on shapes, not details.

Key Insight: You're not learning to draw on paper forever — you're building the visual understanding that makes digital painting possible. Artists who skip this step struggle for years with digital tools because they never learned to see properly. Four weeks of pencil sketching saves you months of frustration later.
Phase 2

Go Digital — Your First Tablet

Weeks 5–12 · Your first purchase

After a month of pencil practice, your hand understands the basics of mark-making. Now it's time to go digital. You need a drawing tablet — this is the single most important purchase on your journey. A mouse cannot create art with the sensitivity you need; a tablet stylus detects how hard you press (pressure sensitivity), which lets you make thick and thin lines, soft and hard brushstrokes, just like a real brush.

Don't buy an expensive tablet yet. At this stage you're learning coordination — drawing on a surface while looking at your Mac's screen. It takes about a week to get comfortable, and a budget tablet does this job perfectly.

What to Buy Now

XPPen Deco 01 V3 (screenless tablet) ~₹4,000
OR Ugee M908 (larger work area) ~₹3,500
OR Wacom One S (smaller, trusted brand) ~₹4,500

All three options are screenless tablets — you draw on the flat surface while looking at your Mac's screen. They all come with a battery-free stylus and work with macOS. The XPPen Deco 01 V3 is the best all-rounder with 16,384 pressure levels, eight shortcut keys, and Mac support out of the box. Install the tablet's driver from the manufacturer's website before plugging it in.

Software to Install (All Free)

Krita (Mac) — your main painting studio
Free, open-source, professional-grade digital painting app. 100+ brushes, layers, color management, animation tools. Download from krita.org. This is where you'll spend most of your time.
Autodesk Sketchbook (Mac) — quick sketching
Free, minimal, fast. Great for quick sketches and warm-ups. Very clean interface that won't overwhelm a beginner.
AmberPaint (Android phone) — animated art
The viral animated-brush app you saw. Free on Play Store. Use your finger to create animated drawings. Fun for social media content while you build your skills.
AmberDraw (Mac App Store) — animated art on desktop
The iOS/Mac version. Runs on your M4 Mac. Better experience with a tablet stylus once you have one.

What to Study

Krita beginner tutorials on YouTube — Search "Krita beginner tutorial 2026." Learn the interface: how brushes, layers, and the color picker work. David Revoy's Krita tutorials are especially good.

Continue Ctrl+Paint — Move to the "Digital Painting 101" track now that you have a tablet. These lessons translate directly to Krita.

Study values before color. Paint only in black and white for your first two weeks digitally. This forces you to understand light and shadow without the distraction of color. It's the most important skill in painting.

Daily Practice — 45 Minutes to 1 Hour

10 min: Warm-up sketches in Sketchbook — loose gesture drawings, quick shapes.

20 min: Structured study in Krita — follow a tutorial or practice a specific skill (blending, value scales, basic forms like spheres, cubes, and cylinders with light and shadow).

15–30 min: Paint something from a photo reference. Start with simple objects: an apple, a cup, a still life. Focus on getting the values (light and dark areas) right before worrying about detail.

The hand-eye disconnect is normal. Drawing on a flat tablet while looking at your screen feels strange at first. Everyone goes through this. By day 5–7 it starts feeling natural. By week 3 you won't even think about it. Don't give up during those first few awkward days.
Phase 3

Build Real Skills — Color, Composition, Style

Months 3–8 · This is where you grow fastest

By now you can navigate your tablet, your software, and basic forms. This is the longest phase and the most rewarding one — it's where your paintings start going from "okay" to "wow, you made that?" You'll learn color theory, composition (how to arrange things in a painting so they look pleasing), and start developing your personal style.

At this stage, increase your practice to one hour a day, or more on weekends. Consistency matters far more than marathon sessions — painting for one hour every day beats painting for seven hours on Sunday.

What to Study

Color theory: Learn the color wheel, warm vs cool colors, complementary colors, and how to create mood with color. Marco Bucci's "10 Minutes to Better Painting" YouTube series is exceptional and free — watch all 10 episodes.

Composition: Learn the rule of thirds, leading lines, focal points, and visual flow. These rules determine whether a painting feels balanced or chaotic.

Master studies: Pick paintings you love by artists you admire. Try to recreate them in Krita. This isn't copying — it's how every great painter in history learned. You'll absorb their techniques for handling light, color, and brushwork.

Anatomy basics (if you want to paint people): Proko's free anatomy series on YouTube, or the book "Figure Drawing for All It's Worth" by Andrew Loomis (available free as a PDF online).

Daily Practice — 1 Hour+

10 min: Gesture drawing warm-ups. Quick 30-second to 2-minute sketches of poses (use quickposes.com or Line of Action for timed reference photos).

20 min: Focused study — pick one skill per week. Week 1: skin tones. Week 2: painting clouds. Week 3: fabric folds. Week 4: reflective surfaces. Go deep on one thing rather than shallow on everything.

30+ min: Work on a personal painting project. Pick a subject you love and take it from sketch to finished piece. Having a project gives your practice purpose and direction.

📖 Books to Read in This Phase

"Color and Light: A Guide for the Realist Painter"
by James Gurney
Considered the painter's bible. It explains how light actually works in the real world — how sunlight differs from indoor light, how colors shift in shadow, how atmosphere affects distant objects, how reflections behave. Once you read this, you'll start seeing light differently in your everyday life, and your paintings will immediately improve. Beautifully illustrated throughout. This is the most important art book you will ever own.
~₹1,500
"Figure Drawing for All It's Worth"
by Andrew Loomis
Read this when you want to draw people. Loomis breaks down the human body into simple, constructable shapes with an almost mathematical approach that makes it very learnable. A classic that every figure artist has studied. Available as a free PDF online, or buy a physical copy to keep on your desk.
Free (PDF) / ~₹800 (print)

Optional Upgrade — Month 5 or 6

Ugee UE16 pen display (draw on screen) ~₹16,000
OR XPPen Artist 13 (2nd Gen) ~₹18,000

A pen display lets you draw directly on the screen, which feels much more natural — like drawing on paper. This is an optional upgrade. If your budget is tight, your screenless tablet works perfectly fine and many professional artists use screenless tablets their entire career. But if art is becoming a serious passion by month 5–6, a pen display is a wonderful investment. The Ugee UE16 is the best value: 15.4-inch Full HD screen for under ₹16,000. It connects to your Mac and works as both a drawing surface and a second display.

Start sharing your work. Create an Instagram art account. Post your progress. The community is incredibly supportive toward growing artists. Sharing holds you accountable and builds an audience. Use AmberDraw/AmberPaint to add animated effects to finished paintings before posting — this is exactly how the reel you saw was made.

Phase 4

Refine, Specialize, Create Beautiful Work

Months 9–18+ · Developing mastery

This is where everything comes together. You have strong fundamentals, you're comfortable with your tools, and you understand color and composition. Now the focus shifts to developing your own voice as an artist and painting at the level that made you fall in love with art in the first place.

Pick a specialty — the genre of art that excites you most. Portraits, landscapes, fantasy environments, character design, still life, anime-style illustration, concept art, botanical paintings. Going deep in one area lets you develop mastery faster than staying broad. You can always expand later.

Deepening Your Knowledge

Take a paid course if ready: Schoolism.com, Domestika, and Skillshare have excellent intermediate-to-advanced digital painting courses. A single Domestika course costs around ₹400–800 on sale and can accelerate your growth significantly.

Study from life. Set up small still-life scenes — a lamp lighting a few objects on a table — and paint them from observation. Nothing teaches you light and color faster than painting what's actually in front of you.

📖 Books for This Phase

"Imaginative Realism: How to Paint What Doesn't Exist"
by James Gurney
The companion to "Color and Light." Now that you understand how light works, this book teaches you how to paint scenes from your imagination — fantasy worlds, creatures, environments that don't exist in real life — and make them look utterly convincing. Essential if you want to create original concept art or illustration.
~₹1,500
"Figure Drawing: Design and Invention"
by Michael Hampton
Takes figure drawing further than Loomis. Hampton focuses on understanding the body as a design — how to simplify, exaggerate, and bring life to your characters. Excellent for anyone serious about drawing people, characters, or animation.
~₹2,000

Worth Considering at This Stage

Clip Studio Paint Pro (one-time purchase) ~₹4,200
"Color and Light" by James Gurney (book) ~₹1,500
iPad + Apple Pencil (if budget allows) ~₹35,000+

Clip Studio Paint is the most popular paid software among digital illustrators — smoother brushes, better line stabilization, and outstanding ink and comic tools. Krita is still excellent and many professionals use it exclusively, so this is optional. An iPad with Apple Pencil is the ultimate portable painting setup, but it's a luxury, not a necessity — everything you need is already on your Mac with a tablet.

Daily Practice — 1–2 Hours

Quick studies: Paint small studies from photo references in 30–45 minutes. Speed painting teaches you to prioritize what matters and let go of perfectionism.

Finished pieces: Dedicate 3–5 sessions per painting to create polished work. Take a piece from thumbnail sketch → rough composition → refined drawing → color blocking → rendering → final details.

Community and feedback: Join art Discord servers or Reddit's r/learnart and r/DigitalPainting. Getting feedback on your work from other artists accelerates improvement dramatically.

The Truth About Getting "Very, Very Good"

Getting good at painting is not about talent — it's about mileage. The artists whose work takes your breath away have drawn thousands of bad paintings to get there. The only difference between you and them is that they started and didn't stop.

Here's a realistic timeline: after three months of consistent daily practice, your work will look recognizably competent. After six months, friends and family will be impressed. After a year, you'll be producing work that gets real engagement on social media. After two years of serious practice, you'll be creating paintings that look professional.

The secret that no tutorial tells you: the first 50 paintings are just the price of admission. Don't judge your ability by any single painting. Judge it by the gap between painting number 1 and painting number 50. That gap will astonish you.

Start today. Grab whatever pencil is nearest to you, open a blank page, and draw something you can see right now. That's the first step on your roadmap. Everything else follows.
