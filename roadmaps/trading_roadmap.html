Your Personal Roadmap

From Zero to Successful Trader

A phased plan for learning intraday and swing trading in the Indian stock market — what to study, what tools to use, when to start with real money, and how to survive.

⚠ An Honest Warning Before You Begin

SEBI data shows that roughly 90% of individual intraday traders in India lose money. This roadmap exists to put you in the other 10%, but only if you follow it with discipline. The traders who survive are the ones who spent months learning and paper trading before risking a single rupee. Skipping straight to real money is the fastest way to lose it. Treat this like a serious education — because it is one. This roadmap is educational guidance, not financial advice. Always do your own research before making investment decisions.

Phase 2

Learn Technical Analysis — The Language of Charts

Weeks 7–14 · Cost: ₹0–1,500 · Still no real trading

Technical analysis is how traders read charts to predict where a stock's price might go next. It's based on the idea that price patterns repeat because human psychology repeats. Candlestick patterns, support and resistance levels, volume, trends, and indicators — these are the tools you'll use every single day as a trader. This phase is where you become fluent in chart reading.

You don't need to master every indicator ever invented. Most successful intraday and swing traders use just 3–5 tools consistently. The goal is to deeply understand a few tools rather than vaguely know many.

Master these core concepts first: Candlestick patterns (Doji, Hammer, Engulfing, Morning Star, Evening Star), Support and Resistance, Trendlines, Volume analysis, Moving Averages (20 EMA, 50 EMA, 200 SMA), RSI (Relative Strength Index), VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price).

Phase 3

Paper Trading — Practice Without Risk

Months 4–6 · Cost: ₹0 · Your most important phase

This is the phase that separates the traders who survive from the ones who blow up their accounts in the first month. Paper trading means placing trades with virtual (fake) money using real market data. The prices move exactly like the real market — the only difference is that your money isn't on the line. Think of it as a flight simulator for traders.

You need to paper trade for at least 2–3 months and show consistent profitability before you put real money in. This isn't optional — it's the most important rule in this entire roadmap. Many traders skip this step because it's "boring" compared to real trading. Those are the same traders who lose ₹50,000 in their first month and quit forever.

🎯 Start with Swing Trading — NOT Intraday

This is critical. Begin your trading practice with swing trading (holding positions for 2–15 days), not intraday (buying and selling on the same day). Swing trading gives you more time to think, analyze, and make decisions without the pressure of minute-by-minute price changes. It's much more forgiving for beginners.

Move to intraday only after you've been consistently profitable in swing trading for at least 2 months. Intraday trading requires faster decision-making, tighter risk management, and emotional control that only comes with experience.

Daily Practice — 2 Hours

Before market opens (8:45–9:15 AM): Pre-market analysis. Check overnight global cues (US markets, SGX Nifty). Identify 3–5 stocks that match your setup criteria. Mark your entry, exit, and stop-loss levels BEFORE the market opens. Write them down.

During market hours (9:15 AM – 3:30 PM): Place your paper trades. Follow your plan — don't deviate. If you said you'd exit at ₹520, exit at ₹520. For swing trades, check once at open and once before close.

After market closes (evening, 30 min): Review every trade in your trading journal. Write down: what you traded, why you entered, why you exited, what went right, what went wrong, and what you'd do differently. This journal is your most powerful learning tool.

Phase 4

Start Small with Real Money — Swing Trading First

Months 7–12 · Start with capital you can afford to lose completely

You've proven profitability on paper. Now it's time to trade with real money — but in the smallest possible size. This phase exists because trading with real money feels completely different from paper trading. When your own ₹5,000 is on the line, emotions you never felt during paper trading will surface: fear of loss, greed when a trade is working, panic when a trade goes against you. You need to experience and master these emotions with small amounts before you scale up.

💰 Capital Rules — Non-Negotiable

Start with ₹10,000–25,000. This is your "tuition fee" capital. You must be mentally prepared to lose all of it. If losing ₹25,000 would cause financial stress, start with ₹10,000 or less.

Never trade with borrowed money. No loans, no credit cards, no money you need for rent, food, or emergencies. Ever.

Risk only 1–2% of your capital per trade. With ₹25,000, that's ₹250–500 maximum risk per trade. This means your stop loss should be set so that if it triggers, you lose no more than ₹250–500. This single rule prevents you from blowing up your account.

Start with equity delivery and swing trades only. No intraday, no F&O, no options in this phase. Equity delivery trades have no brokerage on Zerodha and no risk of being forced out of a position by the end of the day.

Your Trading Routine

Sunday evening (1 hour): Weekly analysis. Review the Nifty 50 and your watchlist of 15–20 stocks. Identify which stocks are at interesting support/resistance levels, showing clean chart patterns, or have upcoming catalysts. Shortlist 3–5 potential trades for the week.

Every trading day — before market opens (15 min): Check global cues, review your shortlist, decide if any setups are triggered. Write down your plan: entry price, stop loss, target, position size.

During market hours: Execute your plan. For swing trades, you don't need to watch the screen all day. Place your orders (with stop loss) and check once midday and once before close.

Every trading day — after market closes (15 min): Update your trading journal. Record every trade, every emotion, every deviation from your plan.

Friday evening (30 min): Weekly review. Calculate your weekly P&L, win rate, and average risk-reward. Look for patterns in your mistakes.

Phase 5

Level Up — Intraday Trading and Scaling

Month 12+ · Only after consistent swing trading profits

If you've been consistently profitable in swing trading for at least 3–4 months with real money, you're ready to explore intraday trading. Intraday means you buy and sell within the same trading day — all positions are closed before 3:30 PM. It's faster, more intense, and requires a different mindset. You need to be comfortable making quick decisions and accepting small losses immediately.

This is also when you can start cautiously exploring F&O (Futures & Options) if you're interested, but only after thorough study of Zerodha Varsity Modules 5 and 6 and extensive paper trading of F&O specifically.

Intraday-Specific Skills to Build

Timeframes: Switch from daily charts to 5-minute and 15-minute charts for intraday. Use the daily chart for trend direction, then the 15-minute chart for entries.

Key indicators for intraday: VWAP (the single most important intraday indicator — institutional traders use it as a benchmark), 9 EMA and 20 EMA on the 15-minute chart, RSI for overbought/oversold conditions, and Volume for confirmation.

Price action at key levels: Support/resistance, previous day's high and low, opening range (first 15–30 minutes). Most profitable intraday trades happen at these levels.

Zerodha Varsity — Module 9: Risk Management — Covers portfolio variance, position sizing, and the psychology of risk. Critical before you scale up capital.

🛡 Intraday Risk Management Rules

Set a daily loss limit. If you lose ₹1,000 in a day (or whatever your limit is), stop trading for the day. Walk away. No "revenge trading" to make it back — that's how small losses become catastrophic ones.

Maximum 2–3 trades per day. Overtrading is the #1 killer of intraday accounts. Quality over quantity. Wait for your setup, take the trade, manage it, done.

Always use a stop loss. Never, ever trade without a stop loss. Not "mental" stop losses — actual stop-loss orders placed in the system. The market doesn't care about your feelings.

Avoid the first 15 minutes. The market open (9:15–9:30 AM) is extremely volatile and unpredictable. Let the market settle, observe the opening range, then look for your setup.

The Golden Rules of Trading

Print these out. Stick them next to your screen. Read them before every trading day. Every experienced trader learns these the hard way — by losing money. You can learn them now, for free.

Rules That Protect Your Capital

1. Never risk more than 1–2% of your total capital on a single trade. This is the single most important rule in all of trading. It ensures that no single bad trade can destroy your account. With ₹50,000, that's ₹500–1,000 maximum loss per trade.

2. Always use a stop loss. Decide your exit point before you enter. Place the stop-loss order immediately after entering the trade. Never move it further from your entry to "give the trade more room" — that's hope, not strategy.

3. Risk-reward ratio of 1:2 minimum. Only take trades where your potential profit is at least twice your potential loss. If your stop loss is ₹10 away from entry, your target should be at least ₹20 away.

4. Never average down on a losing trade. Adding money to a losing position is how small losses become account-destroying losses. If a trade isn't working, cut it and move on.

5. Keep a trading journal. Every trade, every day. Entry, exit, reason, emotion, result. Review weekly. The patterns in your mistakes are more valuable than any strategy.

Rules That Protect Your Mind

6. Never revenge trade. After a loss, your brain wants to make it back immediately. That impulse leads to the worst trades. Take a break. Walk away.

7. Don't trade when emotional. Angry, anxious, overconfident, euphoric after a big win — these states lead to impulsive decisions. Trade when you're calm and clear.

8. Accept that losses are part of the game. Even the best traders lose 40–60% of their trades. What makes them profitable is that their wins are larger than their losses.

9. Don't follow tips from social media. If someone on Twitter or Telegram is telling you to buy a stock, ask: why are they telling you for free? Develop your own analysis. Your own conviction is the only thing that will keep you disciplined during a drawdown.

10. Protect your capital above everything. You can always find another trade. You can't trade without capital. Survival comes first, profits come second.

The Truth About Becoming a Successful Trader

Most people come to trading hoping to get rich quickly. The truth is the opposite — trading is a skill that takes 1–2 years of disciplined study and practice to develop. It's more like learning a musical instrument than buying a lottery ticket.

The traders who make it are not the smartest or the luckiest. They're the most disciplined. They follow their rules, they manage their risk, they cut losses quickly, they let winners run, and they never stop learning. They treat trading as a business, not a casino.

Here's a realistic timeline: in the first 6 months, focus entirely on learning — not making money. In months 6–12, start small and expect to break even at best. In year 2, if you've been consistent and disciplined, you'll start seeing meaningful profits. By year 3, you'll know whether trading is your path — and if it is, you'll have the skills to do it well for the rest of your life.

The best time to start learning is now. Open Zerodha Varsity, read Chapter 1, and begin. Your future self will thank you for the months you spent learning before you traded your first rupee.
