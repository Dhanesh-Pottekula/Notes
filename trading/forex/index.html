Forex, from zero to trading it well

Eight phases, from setting up to trading small with real money and then specialising. Each phase has a goal, what to learn, the best resources for it, a hands-on task, and a check that tells you when to move on. Below the phases is a routine, the mistakes that end accounts, and a searchable library of everything found.

The phase lengths and move-on checks are a proposal: no credible source gives numbers for these. Every resource has a badge: checked link confirmed (page opened, or returned by a search with matching details), partly only a summary or secondary page backs the details, unverified from memory — check before relying on it. The base rates

Most retail currency traders lose. These numbers don't say you will, but they set the bar: a strategy must beat costs and beat what most people do.

61–66%of active US accounts at one dealer were unprofitable in each quarter, Q4 2025 – Q3 2026tastyfx disclosure · one quarter at a time
74–89%of EU retail CFD accounts lost money (before the 2018 leverage caps)ESMA 2018 · 12-month basis, not comparable with the US figure
97%of people who day-traded 300+ days lost money; 1.1% beat minimum wageBrazil, index futures — not FX
~7%of 100,000 prop-firm traders ever received a payoutFPFX data via Finance Magnates · one vendor, not audited
And one piece of good news

Leverage causes losses, not just goes with them. After the US capped retail leverage at 50:1 in 2010, US traders did better than uncapped EU traders, mainly because they stopped holding losers (Heimer & Imas, RFS 2022). After Australia's caps, losing accounts fell from 64% to 50% a quarter (ASIC). Low leverage is the cheapest edge you can buy.

PHASE 0week 1

Set up and ground rules

Goal: know the rules of the venue you'll trade on, open free demo accounts, and build a filter for scams before you spend anything.

Trading from the US: how it works

TopicWhat applies
Who can hold your moneyOnly CFTC-registered dealers (FCM/RFED). In Sep 2026 six firms held all US retail forex money, about $470m: FOREX.com (GAIN Capital), OANDA, Charles Schwab Futures and Forex, tastyfx (formerly IG US), Interactive Brokers, Trading.com. Anything else offering spot FX or CFDs to US residents is offshore.
LeverageMinimum deposit 2% on major pairs (50:1) and 5% on all others (20:1). NFA's major currencies: USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, CAD, AUD, NZD, SEK, NOK, DKK. For crosses between two of them (e.g. GBP/JPY), check your dealer's margin table. (EU is 30:1 on majors.)
FIFO, no hedgingNFA Rule 2-43: a dealer can't carry offsetting positions in one account, and closes go first-in, first-out. Strategies or EAs that open buy and sell on the same pair at once won't work as written.
No CFDsUS retail can't trade CFDs, so index/commodity CFD content in UK/EU books doesn't apply.
PlatformsMT5: tastyfx and FOREX.com. OANDA US: MT4 + TradingView + its own API (no MT5). cTrader: no US-regulated broker found. TradingView connects to FOREX.com, OANDA, tastyfx, IBKR.
GoldFOREX.com is reported to offer spot XAU/USD to US clients (not confirmed on its US site; OANDA US not confirmed). The exchange route is CME gold futures, including Micro Gold. Check before you build gold EAs for a US account.
Exchange alternativeCME micro FX futures: Micro EUR/USD (M6E) = 12,500 EUR, $1.25 a tick. Centrally cleared, real volume, no dealer on the other side, no daily swap (carry is in the price; contracts roll quarterly).
Each dealer's report cardUS dealers must publish what share of accounts were profitable each quarter. Read it before you open an account.
Scam filter
  • The FTC has acted against trading "education" sellers: Online Trading Academy (2020 settlement, $362m judgment) and IM Mastery Academy / IYOVIA (2025–26; alleged harm over $1.2bn, defendants banned from selling trading training). Both used earnings claims.
  • CFTC red flags: guaranteed or "no-loss" returns, pitches to trade "the interbank market", pressure to send money fast, backgrounds you can't check, paid signal groups (Telegram/Discord), recruiting others, "recovery" services for people who already lost.
  • Check any firm or person on NFA BASIC. Ask any vendor for a verified Myfxbook or FX Blue link. No link, no money.
    Do
    • Open free demo accounts at two US dealers: OANDA (for its Python API) and tastyfx or FOREX.com (for MT5).
    • Set up TradingView (free), the Forex Factory calendar, and a trade journal (a spreadsheet or your own Python script is fine).
    • Read one dealer's quarterly profitability disclosure and write one line on what it means.
    Move on when

    You have two demo logins working, a journal template, and you can say the leverage cap and FIFO rule from memory. (proposal)

    PHASE 1weeks 1–3

    Mechanics and market structure

    Goal: quote any pair, size any position, know what each trade costs, and understand who is on the other side.

    ConceptIn plain words
    Pair, base, quoteEUR/USD = 1.0850: 1 euro (base) costs 1.0850 dollars (quote). Buying the pair = buying euros, paying in dollars.
    Majors, crosses, exoticsMajors contain USD (EUR/USD, USD/JPY, GBP/USD, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, NZD/USD). Crosses don't (EUR/GBP, GBP/JPY). Exotics pair a major with a smaller currency: wider spreads, sudden jumps.
    Bid, ask, spread, commissionYou sell at the bid, buy at the ask. Spread-only accounts hide the cost in the spread; RAW/core accounts show a tighter spread plus a commission (e.g. $5–7 per $100k per side).
    Pip, lot, pip valuePip = 0.0001 (0.01 for JPY pairs). Standard lot = 100,000 units. EUR/USD: $10 a pip per lot. Use the calculator below.
    Leverage vs margin vs riskMargin is the deposit; leverage sets how small it is. Risk is set by position size and stop. See the leverage widget.
    Swap / rolloverPositions held past 5 pm New York pay or earn the rate gap between the two currencies, usually triple on Wednesday night (spot settles in two days). Free backtesting libraries ignore it — you must model it.
    Spot, forward, swap, future, option, NDFSpot settles in 2 days. A forward is spot adjusted by the rate gap. FX swaps are the biggest product (42% of turnover); spot is 31%. Futures are exchange-traded forwards. NDFs settle in dollars for restricted currencies.
    Who trades$9.5tn a day (BIS, April 2025). Inter-dealer 46%, other financial institutions 50%, companies 5%. UK ~38% of trading, US ~19%. 59% electronic.
    Your broker is not the marketDealers internalise over 80% of customer trades. Many liquidity providers use "last look" (FX Global Code, Principle 17). Every broker's prices — and candles — differ slightly.
    Tick volumeNo central exchange, so MT5 "volume" counts price changes, not size. CME futures volume is the real-volume stand-in.

      PHASE 2weeks 3–8

      Macro and central banks

      Goal: explain why a currency moved today, and say what is already priced in before the next central-bank meeting or data release.

      DriverHow it moves a currency
      Rates and central banksThe biggest driver in G10. What moves price is the surprise against what's expected, not the decision. Market-implied odds (FedWatch, RateProbability) tell you what's priced.
      Interest-rate parityCovered parity (forward = spot + rate gap) mostly holds; it has had small deviations since 2008 (Du, Tepper & Verdelhan). Uncovered parity fails — high-rate currencies don't fall as much as they "should" (Fama 1984). That failure is the carry trade.
      Forecasting is hardA random walk forecasts exchange rates about as well as structural models (Meese & Rogoff 1983). Fundamentals explain direction over months, not the next hour.
      Inflation, real rates, growthReal (inflation-adjusted) rates matter more than headline rates. Learn NFP, CPI, GDP, PMIs: actual vs forecast vs previous.
      Risk-on / risk-offIn panics money runs to USD, JPY, CHF; in calm markets AUD, NZD and EM currencies tend to rise.
      CommoditiesCAD and NOK follow oil, AUD follows iron ore and China, NZD follows dairy. Gold competes with the dollar and real yields.
      The dollar's roleUSD is on one side of 89% of trades and 56.7% of global reserves (IMF COFER, Q2 2026). "Dollar strength" mostly means euro weakness in the DXY index.
      Flows and interventionOrder flow explains much of daily moves (Evans & Lyons). Month-end hedging flows move FX at the London 4 pm fix. BoJ intervention, the SNB's 2015 floor removal.
      How pros learn macro

      Adam Button (ForexLive): learn one currency and its country deeply, then use it as a template; read central-bank speeches and data releases in full, not commentary. Brent Donnelly has written a daily market note for years — the habit is the training.

        The 11 central banks to follow (official pages)
          Do
          • Pick one pair (EUR/USD or USD/JPY). Every trading day for four weeks, write a 5-line note: what's priced in for each central bank, the next catalyst, positioning (COT), your view, and what would prove you wrong.
          • Before each big release, write what you expect; check what the pair did in the next hour.
          Move on when

          Four weeks of notes with no gaps, and you can explain the last three big moves in your pair from rates and data. (proposal)

          PHASE 3weeks 6–10

          Price, flow and positioning

          Goal: apply the technical analysis you already know to FX's quirks, and add positioning and flow timing.

          Your pages (reuse, don't relearn)
          • Trend, levels, patterns: Murphy ch.4, ch.5, ch.6; Grimes ch.1–ch.8.
          • Intermarket: Murphy ch.17. COT: Murphy ch.7. Moving averages: notes, Grimes ch.14.
          • Sessions and opening ranges: Grimes ch.15, Carter.
          • Flow and volume: order flow, VWAP + volume profile — remember FX volume is tick volume.
          • 24 hours, 5 days: the "daily close" depends on the broker. Use New York-close candles (5 a week).
          • Levels that matter in FX: session highs and lows, the Asian range, the previous day's high/low, round numbers (1.1000, 150.00) where orders and option expiries cluster.
          • Positioning: the CFTC Traders in Financial Futures report splits CME currency positions into dealers, asset managers and leveraged funds. Crowded trades unwind violently.
          • Timing flows: month-end and the London 4 pm fix (Evans 2018; Melvin & Prins 2015).
          • Does TA work in FX? Professionals use it widely (Menkhoff & Taylor 2007). Test every pattern yourself — most "smart money" vocabulary renames ideas in Wyckoff and Grimes.
            Do

            Use bar replay (TradingView or FX Replay) on 3 months of your pair's 15-minute chart. Write each setup as a "play": context, trigger, stop, target, and what invalidates it. Keep only plays you can describe in rules.

            Move on when

            You have 2–3 written plays with exact rules a computer could follow. (proposal)

            PHASE 4months 2–4

            Strategy research and honest backtesting

            Goal: find one or two edges that survive all costs, out-of-sample data and nearby settings. This is where an engineer has a real advantage.

            FamilyIdeaEvidence
            CarryLong high-rate, short low-rate currencies; collect the swap.papers A priced risk factor (Lustig, Roussanov & Verdelhan 2011), but negative skew — it crashes when funding dries up (Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen 2008). Weaker since ~2010 (Robot Wealth).
            Momentum / trendBuy currencies that rose over 1–12 months.papers Real and largely unrelated to carry, but concentrated in costly currencies (Menkhoff et al. 2012). CTA trend followers trade it in futures.
            ValueFundamentals-adjusted real exchange rates mean-revert.papers Predicts returns (Menkhoff et al. 2017); also in Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013. Slow — months to years.
            Flow timingMonth-end and 4 pm fix effects.papers Documented (Evans 2018; Melvin & Prins 2015). May have been arbitraged since; test.
            Session breakoutBreak of the Asian range at London, or London range at New York.untested Common in retail EAs. Any edge is likely small; spread and fill timing decide it.
            NewsTrade the release or the move after.untested Spreads widen and last look rejects fills exactly then. Safer as a filter.
            Grid / martingaleAdd to losers until price comes back.avoid Smooth until one long move wipes the account.

            Backtest rules (Carver, Robot Wealth, MT5 docs)

            1. Model every cost: spread at the worst session you trade, commission, and daily swap. No free Python library handles swap for you.
            2. Real ticks for anything intraday: MT5 "Every tick based on real ticks", or Dukascopy bid/ask ticks via Tickstory. Missing ticks fall back to generated ones silently.
            3. Add slippage and delay (0.5–1 pip, more on gold and at news), and test on two data sources.
            4. Few parameters. Prefer settings that work across a range of values over a single best value.
            5. Walk forward: optimise on one block, test on the next, repeat. MT5 only has a single forward split — script the rest.
            6. Benchmark against a plain carry or trend baseline and against random entries with the same exits.
            7. Enough trades: a month of 30 trades says almost nothing. A very high win rate or Sharpe on a short test is a warning sign.
            8. Low turnover: trade only as much as the edge needs; use a no-trade buffer.
              Do
              • Build a G10 carry and a 12-month momentum backtest in Python from FRED rates and daily prices, with costs. These are your baselines.
              • Re-test your existing EAs (in projects/Trading-EA/) on real ticks, 2+ years, with slippage, a second data source and walk-forward. Remove any that use hedged positions if you'll trade a US account (FIFO).
              Move on when

              One strategy has positive expectancy after all costs out of sample, holds up across nearby settings, beats random entries, and has a maximum drawdown you'd accept with real money. (proposal)

              PHASE 52–3+ months · 50–100 trades

              Demo forward test

              Goal: prove you can run the process — routine, execution, journal — exactly as written. Demo profit itself means little.

              • Trade only the plays from Phase 4, at the size the calculator gives, on a US dealer's demo.
              • Journal every trade: setup, reason, screenshot, entry/exit, costs, rule followed (yes/no), emotion.
              • Compare live demo results with the backtest's expected range (win rate, average win/loss, trades per month).
              • Brent Donnelly's numbers: his win rate is 50–53% every year — profit comes from winners being bigger than losers, not from being right more.
                Move on when

                At least 2–3 months and 50–100 trades (whichever comes later), results inside the backtest's range, a complete journal, and zero rule breaks in the last month. (proposal)

                PHASE 66+ months · 100+ trades

                Small live

                Goal: find out whether the edge survives real fills, real swap and your own nerves, with money you can afford to lose.

                • Size: money you can lose. Carver's Starter System needs about $1,500.
                • Leverage: low. Carver suggests about 1.5× effective leverage (brokers allow 50×); the r/Forex wiki suggests roughly 1×. Check "effective leverage" in the widget above.
                • Risk per trade: ≤ 0.5–1% of equity — a common rule of thumb, not a sourced rule.
                • Hard limits: a daily and a monthly loss limit; stop for the day/month when hit.
                • Reviews: monthly and quarterly, as a business. Separate bad luck (variance) from process errors; write a post-mortem for every rule break.
                • Prop challenges are a paid lottery with known odds (about 7% of traders ever paid; FTMO's CEO says 8–10% pass), not a learning stage.
                  Move on when

                  100+ live trades over 6+ months with cost-adjusted returns positive and in line with the backtest, and drawdown inside the planned limit. (proposal)

                  PHASE 7ongoing

                  Specialise and scale

                  Goal: grow slowly and add strategies that don't move together, so one regime change can't end you (Donnelly lost his edge after 2008 because he had only one).

                  • Scale size in steps, only after each review period is good.
                  • Microstructure: order flow, dealer inventory, last look, execution algos.
                  • FX options: volatility, risk reversals, the smile — DeRosa → Jewitt → Wystup or Castagna.
                  • History: Bretton Woods, Plaza, Black Wednesday — regimes repeat.
                  • CME FX futures at depth: Databento data, Sierra Chart, order-book research.
                    ROUTINE

                    Daily, weekly, monthly

                    Built from Rick Wright's 15-step daily checklist (FXStreet), the r/Forex wiki's Knowledge → Analysis → Execution → Review loop, and Brent Donnelly's habits.

                    Before the session

                    1. Am I fit to trade today? Balance and risk per trade checked.
                    2. Open orders and positions checked.
                    3. Economic calendar: what's out today, what's priced in.
                    4. Read the overnight headlines and one bank note (ING FX Daily).
                    5. Dollar index, yields, oil, gold, equities: risk-on or off?
                    6. Higher time frames, then your trading time frame; mark levels.

                    During

                    1. Only plays from your playbook; buy, sell or wait.
                    2. Size with the calculator; stop and target set before entry.
                    3. Decide before any event: exit, trail, or be awake for it.
                    4. No new trades after hitting the daily loss limit.

                    After

                    1. Journal every trade with its reason and a screenshot.
                    2. Log daily P&L (Donnelly has since 2006).
                    3. Bar-by-bar replay of the day: what would the rules have done?
                    4. Check overnight event risk on open positions.

                    Weekly / monthly

                    1. Weekend: next week's calendar and central-bank meetings; COT update.
                    2. Weekly: one-page market note on your pair.
                    3. Monthly: stats by play (win rate, average win/loss, expectancy, costs), rule breaks, post-mortems.
                    4. Quarterly: keep, fix or drop each play. Change size only here.
                    MISTAKES

                    The mistakes that end accounts

                    1. Too much leverage. The causal driver of retail losses (Heimer & Imas; ASIC). Carver's #1 error.
                    2. Overtrading. Costs compound; retail day trading loses after spreads and commissions (Carver).
                    3. Overfitting. Many parameters, one best setting, no out-of-sample test. Black-box ML without basics is "very likely" to overfit (Carver).
                    4. Ignoring swap and spread. Overnight funding is the hidden cost; spreads widen at rollover and news.
                    5. Holding losers, cutting winners (the disposition effect) — the exact behaviour leverage caps reduced.
                    6. Averaging down / grids / martingale. "Never average a losing position" (r/Forex wiki rules).
                    7. Copying others and signal groups. Peers' wins make people trade more, not better (Heimer & Simon).
                    8. Strategy hopping. Learn one method and give it a fair sample.
                    9. One strategy only. Edges die with regimes (Donnelly after 2008).
                    10. Paying gurus. Use the scam filter in Phase 0.
